How to count

Why the defaults are the defaults

It counts the same kinds of thing on both sides.

  • Energy is counted as fuel at the source. The food figures already count the diesel, gas, and electricity used from farm to plate. The game's electricity is multiplied by 1.9, the EU's default for the primary energy each delivered kWh takes.
  • Water is water drawn from rivers and aquifers and not returned: cooling water at the data centre and the power station, and irrigation water for the food. Rain on the fields and water to dilute farm run-off are left out, because the data centre has nothing like them.
  • Both sides count making the thing, not just the last step. The game includes a share of training the model and of making its chips and servers. The chili dog includes raising the cattle, growing the wheat, and processing, shipping, and cooking.
  • Claude's thinking is counted.* Every call's output includes the reasoning written before the answer, and the meter counts all of it, along with everything re-read from earlier in the conversation.

Training the model adds the most energy: two thirds on top of running it, from Google's split of its own machine learning energy. The power station adds the most water: 7.6 L a kWh is a US average from 2003, so it is probably high for today's grid. The roughest part is making the chips and servers, scaled from Mistral's study of its own model, because nobody has published this for Claude.
